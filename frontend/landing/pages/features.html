01 / NODE GRAPH

A financial map you can actually work with.

A node represents one part of your finances: income, checking, savings, investments, a debt, or an expense. Its connections describe how money is routed. Keeping both in the same picture makes it easier to notice competing priorities.

Add a node, enter a balance or monthly amount, and connect it to a destination. Set a connection to a fixed dollar amount or a percentage, then inspect the budget before running a simulation.

  • Separate selection, pan, box-select, and connection tools.
  • Canvas-only fullscreen, zoom, fit view, and automatic layout.
  • Undo and redo, recoverable archived nodes, and JSON import/export.
  • Simulate the full graph or a selected set of nodes.

02 / EVERYDAY MONEY

Understand what is available before investing.

The Overview separates your monthly budget from your current assets and debts. The donut chart lets you switch between these views so a loan balance is never confused with a monthly loan payment.

Budgeting lets signed-in users manage income and expense records, accounts, categories, and recurring entries. Occurrences turn a recurring plan into dated records. These database records and the Node Graph scenario draft are separate workflows; changes are not automatically synchronized between them.

Gross pay can be reduced by an editable effective tax estimate. Income entered as take-home pay is not taxed again. Saving and investing are transfers between your accounts, rather than living costs.

03 / SIMULATIONS

Trade one predicted number for a range of outcomes.

Monte Carlo Simulation runs many possible return sequences through the same financial plan. Historical mode samples twelve-month blocks of monthly returns, keeping some of the market patterns that occurred together.

Markov Regime Simulation models stronger and weaker market states and the chance of remaining in, or switching between, them. Both models use the same cash-flow rules so their differences reflect market paths.

Inspect percentiles, sample paths, the final-outcome distribution, and the year-by-year audit. Change tax, investing, growth, inflation, and return assumptions to understand what moves the result.

01

Holdings with context

Search a ticker, enter shares, and inspect adjusted price history, annualized return, and volatility when enough history is available. Historical prices are cached and are not real-time trading quotes. Holdings do not automatically change the Node Graph simulation.

02

Debt strategies, side by side

Compare avalanche, which targets the highest interest rate, with snowball, which targets the smallest balance. The comparison keeps the same payment budget and reports balances that remain unpaid at the end of the horizon.

03

Your assumptions, visible

Choose the model inputs and inspect the data source. Export a scenario to share its setup with a teammate, or export simulation results to keep a record of the inputs and outcome.
