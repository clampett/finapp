01 / START WITH A SCENARIO

Give each number the right meaning.

Start with the Family demo, or choose a different profile. A demo is an editable example, not a bank connection. Sign in to work with your own profile and use the Node Graph to describe the plan you want to test.

Income and expense nodes are monthly amounts. Checking, savings, investments, and debt nodes are current balances. For example, a $14,000 auto loan balance is different from its $450 monthly payment. Connections and debt-payment overrides tell the model about that payment.

Include the expenses that matter to your household. The model does not invent groceries, insurance, or other bills that you have not entered.

02 / RECONCILE THE BUDGET

Follow the money before following the curve.

For the Family example, $11,250 in gross pay becomes $9,000 after a 20% tax estimate. Subtract $4,600 in living expenses and a $450 loan payment: $3,950 remains after bills.

Of that surplus, the plan routes $1,100 to investments and $300 to savings, leaving $2,550 unassigned. The extra-investing slider applies to that remaining money. At 0%, scheduled investment transfers still apply unless you turn them off explicitly.

Fixed transfers are funded first. Percentage routes share the remainder, and unaffordable allocations are reduced rather than creating extra money. When a loan is paid off, its contractual payment becomes available again.

03 / MONTE CARLO SIMULATION

One plan. A thousand possible journeys.

A Monte Carlo simulation repeats the same plan many times while changing the sequence of market returns. Think of it as asking, “What could happen if this plan experienced many different market histories?” It explores uncertainty instead of giving one certain answer.

The dashboard uses 1,000 simulated paths. Historical mode draws blocks of twelve monthly returns from cached adjusted prices. The selected forward-return assumption can shift the average direction while retaining observed variation. Manual mode uses the return and volatility you enter.

Each path advances month by month: income and bills are applied, affordable transfers are funded, debt payments reduce balances, and asset returns change what remains. The horizon is how long this is repeated.

Historical data may be stale or unavailable. The simulation panel identifies the source, dates, and any fallback assumptions actually used. Past performance is not treated as a promise.

04 / MARKOV REGIME SIMULATION

Account for markets that stay rough for a while.

A Markov regime simulation adds the idea that market conditions can persist. A weak month can be followed by another weak month, rather than every month being independent of the last.

The current model groups historical observations into stronger and weaker states using trailing market direction. It estimates how often the state stayed the same or changed, then samples monthly returns from the appropriate group.

This helps explore sequence-of-returns risk: two plans with similar average returns can finish differently when losses arrive at different times. It does not predict the next regime, identify a future recession, or guarantee that the historical transitions will repeat.

05 / READ THE RESULTS

Know what each line is telling you.

P50, the median: half of modeled outcomes are above this value and half below. It is not a guaranteed or “most likely” personal outcome.

P10, the lower percentile: about 10% of modeled outcomes are lower. It is not a worst-case limit. P90, the upper percentile: about 90% are lower. It is not a maximum.

The percentiles are recalculated at each year, so the lines are summaries across paths rather than three individual journeys. Use the sampled-path view to inspect individual journeys, and the histogram to see the spread at the end.

Inflation-adjusted dollars show purchasing power in today’s terms. Volatility describes how widely returns vary. Net worth means assets minus debt and shortfall borrowing; it can be negative even when the investment balance is positive.

01

When money runs short

The model draws checking first, then savings, then investments. Any remaining shortfall becomes modeled borrowing at the selected APR. Later surplus repays that borrowing before adding new investments. This is a stress assumption, not an offer of credit.

02

Compare against a reference

The cash-flow reference follows the same income, spending, and debt rules with market and cash returns disabled. It helps distinguish the effect of household saving from the effect of returns. It is not the same as total invested capital.

03

Keep the limits in view

The model does not calculate tax brackets, retirement withdrawals, contribution limits, future job loss, or credit limits. Income continues over the chosen horizon unless you change the setup. It is an educational model, not personalized financial advice.
